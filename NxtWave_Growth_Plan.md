# AI Build League — Growth Plan
**500 final-year engineering registrations in 7 days with ₹2,000**

---

## 1. The Student & The Problem

**Target Audience:** Final-year engineering students in India.
**Core Need:** Placement preparation, practical AI exposure, and resume differentiation.

**The Problem:** Most free online workshops are marketed as passive webinars. Final-year students are overwhelmed with generic “Learn AI” ads. They suffer from webinar fatigue and often register without intending to attend.

**The Hypothesis:** Final-year students will respond better to a concrete project outcome and peer recognition than to a generic workshop registration message. 

*Insight:* I didn't want to build another workshop landing page and hope for clicks. Instead, I built a distribution loop around the workshop.

---

## 2. Growth Strategy: The Community Loop

With a constraint of ₹2,000 and 7 days, broad-based paid advertising is not viable. Instead, I chose one high-leverage distribution mechanism: **Student Clubs & Campus Communities**.

Rather than asking clubs to just “share a poster,” the strategy transforms the workshop into a campus-level competition. 

**The Growth Loop (V1 — Initial):**
`CLUB ➔ CAMPAIGN KIT ➔ REGISTRATION ➔ CAMPUS COMPETITION ➔ PROJECT ➔ PROJECT PASSPORT ➔ SHARE ➔ REFERRAL ➔ NEW REGISTRATION`

**The Growth Loop (V2 — Refined):**
`STUDENT CLUBS ➔ CAMPAIGN KIT ➔ CLUB-SPECIFIC LINK ➔ STUDENT REGISTRATION ➔ CAMPUS COMPETITION ➔ PROJECT ➔ PROJECT PASSPORT ➔ SHARE ➔ REFERRAL`

> V2 makes the **Club-Specific Link** an explicit step — this is the attribution mechanism that tracks which club/campus drove each registration and powers the leaderboard competition.

This strategy shifts the burden of acquisition from central paid ads to decentralized community leaders. Every registration becomes an opportunity for another referral through the gamification of the "Project Passport".

## 4. 7-Day Execution Timeline

| Day | Phase | Execution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Day 1** | **Seed** | Recruit initial student clubs. Generate and distribute custom Campaign Kits to club leads. Launch the empty leaderboard. |
| **Day 2** | **Distribution** | Clubs push their unique links to WhatsApp groups. The Campus vs. Campus competition begins. |
| **Day 3** | **Social Proof** | Highlight early registrations. Show leaderboard movement to club leads to instigate FOMO and peer pressure. |
| **Day 4** | **Referral Push** | Students receive referral mechanics in their dashboards. Highlight the fastest-growing campuses. |

| **Day 6** | **Urgency** | "Last 48 hours" messaging. Publish final major leaderboard update to drive final club pushes. |
| **Day 7** | **Final Push** | Leaderboard closing. Final referral push. Confirm workshop participation. |

## 6. Working Asset: The "AI Build League" Prototype

To execute this strategy, I built a live, working web application to automate the growth loop. 

**Key Growth Features Built:**
- **Automated Campaign Kits:** Clubs instantly generate pre-written WhatsApp/LinkedIn templates with their unique tracking links. *(Drives Acquisition)*
- **Registration Funnel:** A frictionless flow where students select their project and campus. *(Drives Activation)*
- **Live Leaderboard:** Ranks campuses and clubs based on a custom "Growth Score" (Registrations + Attendance + Referrals). *(Drives Gamification)*
- **Project Passport:** A dynamic, shareable digital credential generated for students to post on LinkedIn. *(Drives Viral Organic Reach)*
- **Admin Dashboard:** Real-time visibility into the registration funnel, campus performance, and referral rates. *(Drives Monitoring)*

*Every feature exists strictly to move a student to the next stage of the growth loop.*

## 7. Metrics & Experiments

**Primary KPI:** 500 Total Registrations.
**Secondary KPIs:** Registrations per club, Share/Referral rate, Registration-to-Project Selection rate.

To optimize the loop during the 7 days, I propose the following experiments:

**Experiment 1: Individual vs. Community Framing**
- **A:** “Build Your First AI Project in 60 Minutes”
- **B:** “Help your campus build the biggest AI cohort”
- *Measure:* Registration conversion rate & sharing rate.

**Experiment 2: Project-First vs. Workshop-First Messaging**
- **A:** “Attend a free AI workshop”
- **B:** “Choose an AI project and build your first working prototype in 60 minutes”
- *Hypothesis:* The concrete project outcome creates stronger immediate motivation.

## 8. Strategic Conclusion & Key Decisions

**AI Decision Making:** 
When brainstorming, AI initially suggested a broad, multi-channel strategy involving ads, influencers, and email blasts. I rejected this because ₹2,000 and 7 days require extreme concentration, not channel breadth. Furthermore, when AI suggested individual referral payouts (e.g., ₹50 per friend), I pivoted to a community-based leaderboard incentive, which motivates distribution at scale without draining the micro-budget.

**Why this Strategy?**
The constraint was not a lack of marketing ideas; it was ₹2,000 and 7 days. I therefore chose one focused distribution mechanism—student communities—and designed the workshop around a loop that could compound through competition, project sharing, and referrals. 

I didn't build just another workshop landing page. I built a self-sustaining distribution loop around the workshop.
